**Title:** From Forecasts to Decisions: Benchmarking Probabilistic Demand Forecasts for Replenishment and Liquidation on Two Public Retail Datasets

Public retail forecasting benchmarks rank methods by forecast error, yet forecasts are used to make inventory decisions. We benchmark eight probabilistic forecasting methods inside one transparent decision layer: four statistical methods, LightGBM quantile regression, LightGBM-Tweedie with normal safety stock or conformal intervals, and HistGradientBoosting quantile regression. The decision layer combines a multi-period order-up-to policy with optional liquidation and is applied on two public datasets of different retail settings: M5 (brick-and-mortar, 30,381 series) and VN1 (multi-vendor e-commerce, 13,844 series), both at weekly frequency. All series are retained and results are reported by ADI–CV² demand class. Without cost data, methods are compared at equal fill rate along inventory–fill-rate trade-off curves, and liquidation is assessed with a break-even salvage ratio. Every analysis is repeated on a second test window. On M5, LightGBM quantile regression is the most accurate method and needs 4–13% less inventory than strong baselines at fill rates of 0.94–0.96 in both windows. On VN1 it has the lowest mean error, but TSB with negative-binomial demand is as accurate per series, and the most inventory-efficient method changes between windows. Only class-level results are stable: TSB with negative-binomial demand is the most efficient method for smooth series, LightGBM quantile regression for intermittent and lumpy series. A quantile-based liquidation rule costs almost no fill rate, whereas fixed and dead-stock rules raise stockouts. Within 26 weeks, however, liquidation pays off only at salvage prices near unit cost. The results argue for evaluating forecasts at equal service level, by demand class and series by series.

**Keywords:** probabilistic forecasting; inventory management; intermittent demand; quantile regression; LightGBM; liquidation; M5; VN1
